# Superstore Sales Performance - Business Summary

*Period covered: 2015–2018*

## Executive Summary

Revenue grew **50.3%** from 2015 to 2018 ($479.9K → $721.2K), driven mainly by a
handful of strong months each year rather than steady month-to-month growth. Growth is
concentrated in the **West and East regions** and the **Office Supplies and Technology**
categories, while the **South region** and **Furniture** category lag noticeably behind.
The customer base is broad and healthy - no single account represents outsized risk.

## Key Findings

**Overall performance**
- Total sales: **$2.25M** across 9,789 orders and 793 customers over the 4-year period.
- Revenue grew 50.3% from 2015 to 2018, but the typical *individual* month was flat to
  slightly down - growth comes from a few very strong months (see Seasonality below),
  not a broad-based monthly climb.

**Regional performance**
- Ranked by total sales: **West** ($710K) and **East** ($661K) lead, followed by
  **Central** ($493K) and **South** ($389K).
- West is also growing fastest year over year (~21%/year on average), while South is
  growing slowest (~11%/year).

**Category & product performance**
- Technology ($826K), Furniture ($724K), and Office Supplies ($703K) are close in total
  revenue, but **Office Supplies is growing fastest** (~19%/year) while **Furniture is
  growing slowest** (~11%/year) - a signal that today's category rankings may not hold in
  the future.
- At the sub-category level, **Phones and Chairs** are the top sellers; **Fasteners and
  Labels** sell the least - a much wider spread than the category view alone shows.

**Customer base**
- Revenue is not concentrated in a few large accounts: the **top 10 customers account
  for only 6.8%** of total sales. This is healthy from a risk standpoint (no single
  customer's departure would meaningfully hurt revenue), but also means there's no
  obvious "VIP tier" currently driving outsized value.

**Seasonality**
- Sales peak sharply in **September and November–December**, consistent with
  back-to-school and holiday buying. These few months carry a disproportionate share of
  each year's growth.
- More than half of all months actually saw sales *decline* from the month before  - 
  the year-over-year growth story is real, but it's driven by concentrated peaks, not
  consistent month-to-month momentum.

**Shipping & fulfillment**
- Average shipping delay is about 4 days, and scales predictably with the shipping
  option chosen (Same Day ≈ 0 days, up to Standard Class ≈ 5 days).
- Delay differences *between regions* are small (under half a day), suggesting
  fulfillment speed is driven by customer shipping choice, not regional logistics
  problems.

## Forecast (Next Quarter)

A simple forecasting model projects the following monthly sales for Jan–Mar 2019:

| Month | Forecast |
|---|---|
| January 2019 | ~$49,700 |
| February 2019 | ~$42,700 |
| March 2019 | ~$72,600 |

**Caveats:** this is a directional estimate based on only 4 years of history, assumes no
major changes (new stores, promotions, disruptions), and the underlying model had some
difficulty converging during fitting - treat it as a rough size/shape guide for planning,
not a number to commit budgets to.

## Recommendations

1. **Double down on what's working:** West/East regions and Office Supplies/Technology
   categories are outperforming - prioritize inventory and marketing spend there.
2. **Investigate the South region and Furniture category** specifically - understand
   whether the slower growth is a market-size ceiling, a competitive issue, or an
   internal execution gap before deciding whether to invest more or reallocate.
3. **Plan staffing and inventory around September and November–December** specifically,
   since these months carry outsized weight in annual performance; consider targeted
   promotions in the quieter months to smooth revenue rather than relying on year-end
   peaks alone.
4. **Explore upselling faster shipping tiers** - Standard Class (the slowest, ~5 days)
   is likely also the default/cheapest option; since delay is customer-driven rather
   than a regional bottleneck, there may be a straightforward opportunity to promote
   faster paid tiers.
